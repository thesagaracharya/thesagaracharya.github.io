Current research work

The KW51 bridge study — six plates, in the order the analysis actually happened. Move sideways through them; every figure is live.

To move from reading about vibration-based monitoring to doing it, I analysed the KW51 campaign end to end: a steel bowstring railway bridge in Leuven, Belgium, monitored continuously across a documented retrofit. The interesting question is not whether a structural change can be detected — it is whether it can be separated from the weather, and whether the validation is honest enough that a good score could not have come from leakage.

Open data

Maes, K. & Lombaert, G., Monitoring railway bridge KW51 before, during and after retrofitting, KU Leuven — doi:10.5281/zenodo.3745914, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. All processing, analysis, figures and code are my own. KW51 records a known structural state change, not damage labels, so this is framed as structural-condition identification rather than damage detection.

Plate 01 · one recording

A train crosses the bridge in 55 seconds

Vertical acceleration on the deck, sampled at 825.8 Hz. The one-second moving RMS below splits the record into two physically different regimes — and the split is the single most consequential preprocessing decision in the whole study.

Hover or tap the trace to read any instant.
Two regimes, not one. While the train is on the structure the response is driven by the excitation, and the train’s own mass is part of the vibrating system. Once it leaves, the bridge is free to ring at its own frequencies. Treating the record as one signal averages these together — and the loud forced portion wins.
LEADS TO →

If the two windows are physically different, their spectra should look different. Plate 02 checks that, and asks whether the peaks in the quiet half are really the bridge’s modes.

Plate 02 · validation

The quiet half is where the modes are

Normalised spectra of the same recording, computed separately for each window across all six live deck channels. Dashed markers are the campaign’s own tracked modal frequencies for that hour — produced by a different method, by the original researchers, and used here as an independent reference.

Switch windows, toggle channels, hover for the nearest tracked mode.
What the switch shows. The forced window is a thicket of peaks, many of them train forcing and its harmonics rather than structure. The free decay collapses to a handful of persistent peaks that several independent channels agree on — and those line up with the tracked modes. Five of the six channels place mode 9 within one frequency bin of the tracked value.
CAVEAT →

Frequency resolution here is 0.035 Hz in the free window. Agreement closer than that is agreement within one bin — it should not be quoted as three-decimal precision, and it is a consistency check rather than independent modal identification.

Plate 03 · the audit

The instrumentation changed mid-campaign

Quality control on all 60 passage files found six accelerometers absent from every single one, with no partial gaps anywhere. That pattern is unlikely to be a sensor fault, so I checked availability across the whole 15 months.

Hover or tap for monthly values.
The break is in the wrong place for a structural story. Arch modes were tracked before October 2019 — but from deck sensors alone, which is why they were tracked so intermittently. The retrofit ended 27 September 2019; arch instrumentation came online in October 2019. Any before/after comparison on an arch mode therefore compares six sensors against twelve and attributes the difference to the structure.
LEADS TO →

This disqualifies six of the fourteen tracked modes from any before/after comparison — on instrumentation grounds, not on how well they were tracked. Plate 04 works through all fourteen.

Plate 05 · the long view

Fifteen months, five modes, one retrofit

Daily median frequency, plotted as deviation from each mode’s own pre-retrofit level so a 1.9 Hz mode and a 6.4 Hz mode share an axis. Add deck temperature to see what the structural signal has to compete with.

Hover or tap the chart to read any day.
Two signals at once. Modes 6 and 13 settle permanently higher after the works, consistent with added stiffness; mode 9 drops sharply during them and returns slightly below where it started. But the annual sag running through the whole record is temperature, not structure — switch the temperature trace on and the two move against each other.
LEADS TO →

So the structural change has to be separated from the thermal one. I fit a temperature baseline on pre-retrofit data only — a model fitted on data containing the change would absorb it into its own definition of normal. Plate 06 tests whether that baseline still holds afterwards.

Plate 06 · the result

The temperature correction does not transfer equally

Correlation between frequency and temperature after the retrofit — before correction (hollow) and after applying the pre-retrofit baseline (filled). A correction that worked would pull every mode towards zero.

Hover or tap a mode for its values.
Two results worth separating out. Mode 3 correlated negatively with temperature before the retrofit and positively afterwards — a stiffness change alone does not produce a sign reversal. And mode 13’s corrected residual tracks temperature more strongly than the raw frequency did, so the baseline actively mis-corrects it. Reproduced by a second implementation using hourly values and ordinary least squares in place of daily medians and polynomial fitting.
SO WHAT →

Together these suggest the pre-retrofit temperature relationship did not transfer equally across the intervention. A baseline fitted before a structural change cannot be assumed valid after it — and whether it holds differs mode by mode.

Problem
A bridge’s natural frequencies shift when its structure changes — and also when the weather changes, often by more. Detecting a change is easy; proving it is structural, and validating that proof so a high score cannot come from data leakage, is the real difficulty.
Approach
Audited the data before trusting it, then ran two tracks: raw train-passage acceleration processed to spectra and checked against the campaign’s own tracked modal frequencies; and the 15-month modal series normalised for temperature, then used to classify structural state under validation designed to be able to fail.
Methods
Moving-RMS segmentation separating forced response from free decay; zero-phase 4th-order Butterworth band-pass (0.5–30 Hz, second-order sections); FFT and Welch PSD across six live deck channels; peak extraction and matching against tracked modes. For the long series: daily-median aggregation, quadratic temperature baselines fitted on pre-retrofit data only, bootstrap over whole days, model order by information criterion, and logistic-regression classification validated in folds blocked by calendar month with group overlap asserted zero in code.
Results
Found an instrumentation change that disqualifies six of the fourteen tracked modes from before/after comparison. Free-decay peaks agree with independently tracked modal frequencies to within one frequency bin, with five of six channels locating mode 9. Temperature correction shown to be mode-dependent: it transfers well for modes 9 and 6, barely helps mode 3, and actively degrades mode 13. Classification reached 100% on every fold — reported as a saturated, uninformative comparison rather than a success.
Impact
The mode-dependent transfer result bears directly on practice: a baseline fitted before a structural intervention cannot be assumed valid afterwards, and whether it holds differs mode by mode. Delivered as one executed notebook running end to end from the public archive, with eight quality-control assertions, a fixed seed, and results exported to CSV.
On the 100%

The classifier separated before from after with perfect accuracy on every fold, against a majority-class baseline of 61.6%. I report that as a problem, not an achievement: the two states are separated by a frequency gap far larger than the day-to-day spread within either, so the task is trivially separable and both representations sit at the ceiling with zero variance across folds.

With no headroom, the experiment cannot determine whether environmental normalisation helps — which is not the same as showing it is unnecessary. A harder test (training on the coldest months, testing on the warmest) saturated too, until the feature set was cut to single modes; only then did modes 3 and 5 fall to 0.76–0.79 and reveal a mode-dependent structure the combined model hides.

Stated limitations

  • Structural state is confounded with time — before and after are also earlier and later
  • One environmental covariate used; the remaining ten channels are unexploited
  • Daily aggregation reduces but does not eliminate serial dependence
  • The modal check is a consistency check on matched peaks, not independent modal identification — no mode shapes, no damping estimates
  • Perfect separation is within one bridge and establishes nothing about transfer to another structure
  • Frequency change cannot be converted to stiffness change without a validated model and mode shapes

A Self-Calibrating, Cross-Code NDT Framework for Residual-Strength Assessment of Fire-Exposed Structures

Thesis research proposal · sole author · July 2026 · field deployment without coring, validated against laboratory ground truth · full proposal (PDF)

Problem
When a building survives a serious fire, it hides its own damage: concrete can look sound while its core has lost half its strength; steel can stand plumb while its yield capacity has quietly collapsed. The retain–repair–replace decision is one of the highest-stakes judgments in structural engineering, yet visual inspection reads only the surface, and destructive coring — the supposed gold standard — is frequently unsafe, unaffordable, or not permitted on unstable, occupied, heritage, or access-restricted sites. Engineers are forced to choose between a method that is unreliable and one that is unavailable.
Approach
A self-calibrating, cross-code, NDT-only framework. The surviving structure becomes its own calibration standard: every SonReb and hardness correlation is anchored to a minimally-affected reference zone within the same building through a calibration coefficient β, so the largest sources of NDT bias divide out without removing any material. Every element’s residual capacity is then computed twice — under Eurocode 2/3 and under ACI 228/216 — and the divergence between the codes is reported as data, with the governing (lower) result driving the decision. Field deployment is core-free, but the method is validated against genuine ground truth before field use.
Methods
An eight-stage pipeline (Stage 0–7): criteria-based case selection; visual triage (D0–D3) with quantitative reference-zone certification; two parallel NDT suites (UPV, rebound/SonReb, impact-echo, infrared thermography, GPR, half-cell + resistivity for concrete; portable hardness, ultrasonic thickness, MT/DP, and geometric survey for steel); formally defined self-calibration; uncertainty via first-order error propagation and 10,000-draw Monte Carlo simulation with sensitivity analysis, carrying the 5th-percentile lower bound into design; dual-code capacity computation; and a governing demand ratio. A companion laboratory series — specimens cast, heated to controlled temperatures (20–900 °C), tested non-destructively, then crushed — validates the calibrated predictor (R², RMSE, bias, Bland–Altman limits) and benchmarks it against published fire-test data.
Results
Expected outputs: a validated, field-implementable retain–repair–replace decision matrix (Retain: DR ≥ 1.00 · Repair: 0.70 ≤ DR < 1.00 · Replace: DR < 0.70) with an auditable chain from measurement to decision; a first quantified, element-by-element comparison of Eurocode and ACI residual-capacity outcomes on real fire-damaged members; and a rare matched NDT–true-strength dataset. Planned scope: 4–6 fire-damaged case structures (60–120 elements, ≥2 methods each) and ≥30 lab specimens; buildings damaged in the 2025 Nepal unrest form the initial validation set, with selection criteria defined independently of any single site.
Impact
Converts a subjective, high-stakes judgment into an evidence-based, auditable one: avoided over-demolition of salvageable structure, prevention of catastrophic under-assessment, faster reoccupation, and better-targeted repair spend — serving owners, engineers, insurers, regulators, and reconstruction agencies. Proposed as a 12-month thesis; I am seeking faculty supervision, particularly on the four make-or-break challenges the proposal treats explicitly (see appendix).
Technical appendix — pipeline, calibration, thresholds & sampling

Eight-stage pipeline

  • Stage 0 — criteria-based case selection against pre-registered criteria (documented exposure, representative construction type, internal reference zone, safe access, design drawings)
  • Stage 1 — visual triage into damage classes D0–D3 and reference-zone certification by diagnostic thresholds, not visual judgment (surface colorimetry for the >300 °C pink/grey transition, resistivity/half-cell dry-baseline band, UPV + impact-echo soundness)
  • Stage 2 — integrated NDT collection: every element characterized by at least two independent methods (ASTM C597, C805, C1383, C876; single-method results flagged provisional and reduced 15%)
  • Stage 3 — self-calibration: site-corrected readings normalized to the reference zone (V̄ = Vcorr/Vref, R̄ = Rcorr/Rref); β = f′c,ref / SonReb(Vref, Rref) forces the model to reproduce the known reference strength; identical logic maps steel hardness to residual yield
  • Stage 3a — validation against laboratory ground truth and literature benchmarks (Eurocode 2 / ACI-NIST residual factors); only correlations passing both checks proceed to field use
  • Stage 4 — residual properties as distributions: error propagation + Monte Carlo; design value = 5th percentile ≈ f̂′c,res − 1.645 sf
  • Stage 5–6 — dual-code capacity (EN 1992-1-2 / 1993-1-2 vs ACI 228 / 216) on identical inputs; divergence tabulated element by element; governing demand ratio DR = φRn/U
  • Stage 7 — classification with justified thresholds, plus a global ambient-vibration cross-check (measured/estimated first-mode frequency < 0.85 triggers denser testing and a capacity downgrade)

Four make-or-break challenges (supervision sought)

  • Reference-zone certification — fixing the quantitative thresholds (temperature ceiling, resistivity band) that admit a zone as a genuine baseline; near 150 °C, moisture evaporation raises UPV without a matching strength loss
  • Cover vs core under steep gradients — the rebound hammer reads the degraded outer ~30 mm while direct-transmission UPV is dominated by the sound core; treated with impact-echo depth profiling, surface-wave UPV, and thermocouple-instrumented lab gradients
  • Steel hardness vs residual yield — dual mapping of HV against both fy and fu, across air-cooled and water-quenched regimes and stratified by steel grade; doubtful connection members routed to Replace regardless of hardness
  • Lab-to-field restraint & scale transfer — an explicit, one-sided model-error term (may under-predict field capacity, constrained not to over-predict it) injected into the uncertainty budget, cross-checked by ambient vibration

Fallbacks

  • Reference zone fire-affected or absent → hierarchy of external anchors (unheated upper-floor element, protected core, design + mill certificates), else the lab-calibrated correlation with temperature reconstruction and widened uncertainty bounds
  • Unsafe-to-access members conservatively assigned the worst class of their group pending later inspection

DED Cost Estimate — Campus Marga Road, Tilottama Municipality

1.225 km urban corridor (Ch. 0+000 – 1+224.181) · Ward 16, Tilottama, Rupandehi

Problem
Detailed Engineering Design (DED) cost estimation for an urban road corridor procured under RFP No. DUDBC/FPIU/RUPANDEHI/RFP/01/080-81 through the Integrated Urban Infrastructure Development Program (IUIDP), for the Department of Urban Development and Building Construction (DUDBC), Federal Project Implementation Unit, Rupandehi, Government of Nepal.
Approach
As cost estimator, prepared the full pre-construction estimate across 12 major work categories: earthworks, concrete structures, bituminous pavements, drainage, road furniture, and bio-engineering works.
Methods
Earthwork volumes computed by the cross-sectional area method across 63+ chainage stations; quantities estimated for all pavement layers (sub-base, base course, asphalt concrete), covered RCC drains, kerb stones, interlocking block footpaths, and 62 street-lighting poles. All unit rates for labour, materials, and equipment benchmarked against DoR Standard Specifications and DOR Norms (FY 2080/81).
Results
Final Summary of Cost Estimate of approximately NPR 9.47 crore, inclusive of VAT and contingencies.
Impact
A government-procured deliverable feeding directly into IUIDP implementation, with coordinate and design-level control (UTM Easting/Northing and finished road level at every station) for all drainage-invert and furniture-setting calculations.
Technical appendix — geometric design, cross-section & drainage

Geometric design

  • Horizontal alignment — defined by 76 Intersection Points (IPs) with simple circular curves (no spiral transitions); curve radii ranging from 15 m at the tightest urban bends (IPs 22–24, 27–29) to 941 m at the gentlest sweeps (IP 36), reflecting varied urban geometry along the corridor.
  • Vertical profile — extremely flat Terai terrain; elevations rise gently from 117.47 m to 120.02 m, a net gain of roughly 2.35 m over 1.225 km. Of 15 vertical intersection points, only four carry a formed vertical curve (each 20 m long, at VIPs 5, 9, 13, and 14); the remainder are tangent grade changes, consistent with the near-level longitudinal profile.

Cross-section & pavement composition

  • Standard urban cross-section: 3.55 m carriageway on each side (two-lane), flanked by a 0.2 m covered RCC drain and a 0.3 m footpath on both left and right.
  • Normal cross-fall of 2.5% on tangents, transitioning to superelevation at curves — up to 6% on tighter bends and up to 5.5% (adverse-side) at IP 73 — with extra carriageway widening at sharper horizontal curves to maintain safe vehicle tracking.
  • Pavement per DoR specifications for urban bituminous construction: granular sub-base, wet mix macadam base course, and asphalt concrete wearing course.

Drainage & road furniture

  • Covered RCC drains run continuously along both carriageway edges, sized for the urban stormwater load in the flat Terai catchment.
  • Kerb stones delineating the carriageway–footpath interface; interlocking concrete block paving on footpaths; 62 street-lighting poles spaced along the corridor to meet urban illumination standards.

Property & Fixed-Asset Valuation — 53+ Sites Across Nepal

Aakar Engineering Consultancy · land, buildings & fixed assets · NVA-trained (Associate VPO of IVSC)

Problem
Every lending, purchase, or compliance decision on a property ultimately rests on one number — its value — and that number is only defensible when it is built from verified facts on the ground, not from paper claims. Documents overstate, boundaries drift, and construction quality varies member by member.
Approach
End-to-end valuation of more than 53 sites across Nepal through Aakar Engineering Consultancy: detailed physical site inspection, document verification against ground reality, reasoned analysis of land and building value, and a signed report that a bank, buyer, or owner can rely on and audit.
Methods
On-site inspection with physical measurement of land and structures; cross-verification of land-ownership certificates (lalpurja), cadastral maps, and approved building drawings against what actually stands on the ground; boundary, access, road-frontage, and setback checks; assessment of building condition, construction quality, and remaining useful life; land valued from prevailing market evidence alongside government rates, structures by the cost approach with depreciation — concluding in fair market value and distress value for each asset.
Results
53+ completed valuation assignments spanning residential, semi-commercial, and mixed-use properties, each documented as an auditable chain from field measurement and photographic record to concluded value.
Impact
The same verification-first discipline that runs through my research, applied at scale in practice.
